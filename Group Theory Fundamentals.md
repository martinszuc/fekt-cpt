# Group Theory Fundamentals

## Definition of a Group
A group $G$ is a set of elements combined with an operation (like multiplication or addition) that satisfies four properties:
1. **Closure:** If $a, b \in G$, then $a \cdot b \in G$.
2. **Associativity:** $(a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)$.
3. **Identity Element:** There exists an element $e \in G$ such that $a \cdot e = e \cdot a = a$.
4. **Inverse Element:** For every $a \in G$, there is a $b \in G$ such that $a \cdot b = e$.

## Cyclic Groups in Cryptography
In cryptography, we primarily deal with **finite cyclic groups**, denoted often as $\mathbb{Z}_p^*$ (the multiplicative group of integers modulo a prime $p$).

A cyclic group can be generated by a single element. This element is called a generator.
